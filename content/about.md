I am a cloud engineer at [Development Seed](https://developmentseed.org/), based in Durham, UK. Most of my work is about making Earth observation data easier to use: building the pipelines that turn satellite archives into analysis-ready datasets, and contributing to the open-source tools people use to read them.

## What I care about

I am a strong believer in open source and open data. What attracted me to Development Seed is that its projects make scientific data more accessible, build on open standards and open-source tools, and contribute to environmental monitoring, climate research and open science. 

When a tool I depend on has a problem, I would rather fix it upstream than work around it. I have contributed to various projects and tools in the STAC and eoAPI ecosystem, and I started a supply-chain security effort for Development Seed's open-source repositories.

I also like sharing what I learn. Preparing a talk or a workshop is one of the fastest ways I know to find the gaps in my own understanding, and giving it starts conversations that would never have happened otherwise. In 2026 I spoke at FOSS4G Europe and the UK Earth Observation Conference, and helped run an eoAPI + STAC workshop (more on the [talks](/talks/) page). I write about what I learn on [this blog](/posts/).

## What I work on

Since joining Development Seed in November 2025, I have mostly worked on two projects for European agencies.

For ESA's [EOPF Sentinel Zarr Explorer](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/ecosystem/services/eopf-sentinel-zarr-explorer), I worked on the pipelines that converted Sentinel products to GeoZarr and published them in a STAC catalogue, and on the automation that kept a large catalogue in order over time (storage tiers, retention). There is more on the [project page](/projects/eopf-explorer-sentinel-pipelines/).

For EUMETSAT's Destination Earth Data Lake, I co-wrote the first architecture decision record and the initial plan for onboarding the data, then built a large part of the chain that turns Meteosat imagery into Zarr and Icechunk datacubes. I also wrote the quickstart notebooks that users start from.

On both projects, a good part of the work happens before the pipelines are built: agreeing formats and conventions with the agencies that own the data and the teams who build on it, and choosing designs from benchmarks on the real data.

## How I got here

I spent about ten years (2010–2021) as a physical oceanographer. I did an MSc at Université Pierre et Marie Curie (now Sorbonne Université), a PhD at the Université de Perpignan on deep convection and the upper ocean in the Mediterranean, and a short postdoc at LOCEAN in Paris. I then spent four years at the [Scottish Association for Marine Science](https://www.sams.ac.uk/) (SAMS) in Oban and two at the [National Oceanography Centre](https://noc.ac.uk/) in Southampton, working on the North Atlantic Current with gliders and moorings as part of the international [OSNAP](https://www.o-snap.org/) programme.

A lot of that work was making observations usable by other scientists. I merged 13 hydrographic databases into a [Mediterranean climatology](https://doi.org/10.17882/46532) and published it as an open dataset. At SAMS I helped plan and run our part of OSNAP, from the cruises to the archived data: I published the UK-OSNAP glider datasets at the British Oceanographic Data Centre, and adapted and maintained the [mooring processing toolbox](https://github.com/ScotMarPhys/m_moorproc_toolbox) that SAMS still uses on its mooring cruises. The glider and mooring data went into the first OSNAP overturning results, published in Science in 2019, which I co-authored. Along the way I published 30+ peer-reviewed papers, spent about 200 days at sea on 12 research cruises, and learnt a lot about planning and teamwork under pressure.

In 2021 I left research. As a scientist I relied on data systems every day, and I had become more and more curious about how they worked and how to fix them, rather than just reporting issues. I learnt the software side at OSE Engineering, packaging routing algorithms into Python libraries for a delivery-robot prototype, and at the UK Department for Work and Pensions, where I co-architected a secure container platform for data scientists and analysts and mentored junior engineers. At Development Seed, the two sides meet again.

A selection of my papers and datasets is on my [CV](/cv/), and the full list is on [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=10K7fIYAAAAJ&hl=en).
